## Why it mattered

Three separate streams, one building. The truck rental system and the
infrastructure under it. The single sign-on portal every associate used to
reach their tools. And the reporting that told paint department managers how
their department was actually doing.

None of them were the website. All of them were software used by someone
standing on a shop floor with a customer waiting.

## The hard part

I was a contractor, new to the field, working across three teams that did not
report to each other — with no authority in any of them.

That turns out to be a useful constraint to have had early. The only way to get
anything adopted was to make it obviously better and to bring the people who
owned it along, which is a skill I have used in every role since and did not
know I was learning.

### Single sign-on, 2,000+ stores

<Challenge>
  Refine the interface of an integration that let store associates reach
  internal tools quickly and without strain, so they could get back to the
  customer faster.
</Challenge>

A hundred and fifty associates per store, each reaching for internal tools
between customers. The value was measured in seconds recovered, multiplied by a
number that did not fit on a slide. Three things carried it.

### Paint, a two-million-dollar-a-day department

<Challenge>
  Work with the reporting team to turn departmental performance data into
  something a paint manager could act on, rather than something an executive
  could review.
</Challenge>

Not a dashboard for head office. Guidance a department manager could use that
week — and the part that made it usable was ranking the department against
comparable ones rather than against a target in isolation.
