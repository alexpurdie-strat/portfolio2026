## Why it mattered

Three separate streams, one building. The truck rental system, the single
sign-on portal every associate used to reach their tools, and the reporting
that told paint department managers how their department was doing.

None of them were the website. All of them were software used by someone
standing on a shop floor with a customer waiting.

## The hard part

I was a contractor, new to the field, working across three teams that did not
report to each other — with no authority in any of them.

That turns out to be a useful constraint to have had early. The only way to get
anything adopted was to make it obviously better and to bring the people who
owned it along, which is a skill I have used in every role since and did not
know I was learning.

## Key decisions

### Truck rental

> Renting a truck is a long process at the moment.

That was the finding, more or less politely phrased. The work was refining the
rental process for the associates running it, and mapping the infrastructure far
enough out to see where franchising partnerships could attach.

### Single sign-on, 2,000+ stores

A hundred and fifty associates per store, each of them reaching for internal
tools between customers. The work was refining the interface of an integration
that let them get in and get back out — the whole value of it measured in
seconds recovered, multiplied by a number I could not fit on a slide.

### Paint, a two-million-dollar-a-day department

I worked with the reporting team on localized performance insight for paint
managers. Not a dashboard for executives — guidance a department manager could
act on that week.
