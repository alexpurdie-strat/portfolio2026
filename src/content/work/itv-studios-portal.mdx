## Why it mattered

ITV Studios creates, produces and distributes television. The people who deliver
those shows were doing it across a set of processes that had never been designed
as a set — planning, execution and post-transmission each had their own tools,
their own handoffs, and no single view of any of it.

> I've got 9,000 things that have to get done and the one I forget will end up
> being the critical one.

*— A production manager, in research.*

That line is the whole brief. The risk was not that work would be done badly.
It was that the one forgotten thing would be the expensive one.

## The hard part

The obvious product was a dashboard. The actual problem sat upstream of
anything a dashboard could fix.

Partnering with the Studios teams showed that the processes needing influence
were not in our platform at all — they were in how departments handed work to
each other before any of it reached a screen. We could build the best task hub
in the building and it would still be a tidier surface over the same chaos.

### Let the product become service design

As the platform expanded, so did the team's remit, until we had absorbed
products from across the business. We kept optimizing the interface — it went
from nothing to supporting 20+ distinct production-level tasks — but the
larger work moved upstream, into establishing new processes between departments
rather than new screens inside one.

That reached ITV's production partners worldwide, who had been making content
to their own standards and criteria and were now making it to one.
