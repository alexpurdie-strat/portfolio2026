# Positioning

What the site has to demonstrate, and what currently demonstrates it.

Calibrated to a specific target: a staff-level product design role on enterprise
supply-chain platforms at a large CPG business — planning, manufacturing,
logistics and fulfilment, with a design system and AI-enabled products in scope.
Nothing on the site should name or nod at that. The site is a portfolio, not an
application. This file exists so the work selects and frames itself correctly.

## What is actually being screened for

1. **Breadth over depth of ownership.** Operating across product areas rather
   than inside one. "The connective tissue." Evidence of seeing a whole system
   and choosing where design should go next.
2. **Complex operational software.** Internal tools that people use dozens of
   times a day to plan, make, move and deliver things. Multiple user roles.
   Not consumer polish.
3. **Co-owning strategy.** Shaping vision and roadmap alongside product and
   engineering from the start, not receiving it.
4. **Design systems and the governance around them.** Coherence at scale, and
   the practice that keeps it coherent.
5. **AI two ways** — AI-enabled product experiences, *and* AI in the working
   method, with evidence that outputs have changed in the last 18 months.
6. **Practice building and mentorship.** Growing designers; rituals; critique.
7. **Executive storytelling.** Narratives that move senior stakeholders.
8. **Outcomes, not artefacts.** Numbers attached to business consequences.
9. **Where digital meets the physical.** Real operations, real constraints.

## Three conclusions

**Home Depot should come back.** Two thousand stores, ~150 associates each,
truck-rental logistics, single sign-on for internal tooling, decision support
for a department turning over $2M a day, a $5–10M forecast impact and 7m25s
saved per login. Planning, moving and delivering physical goods at national
scale, across multiple roles. It is the single most on-target piece of evidence
available and it is currently not on the site. It fits the Efficiency slot,
which is presently an unwritten entry for an unidentified client.

**Influence gets implied in a line, not documented as a section.** Nothing on
the site currently shows influence, only delivery — but the fix is not a
"how I got buy-in" beat in every entry. An itemised case study reads as
mid-level: it proves the process was followed rather than that the outcome was
owned. It would also contradict the site it sits on, which is unfinished on
purpose. One sentence carries it. "It did not stay a product" says more about
scope and standing than a paragraph of stakeholder narrative would.

The counterweight: the portfolio link travels on a résumé, so it is skimmed
before anyone decides to have the conversation. Outcomes therefore have to be
unmissable. That is a job for the metrics, not for detail.

**The AI story has to be shown, not claimed.** This site is being designed and
built through an AI coding workflow, with a design system, a live annotation
feedback loop and generated assets. That is a working artefact of the thing
being asked about. The seam — metadata written as unrendered source — is the
one place the site already admits it is a made thing, and is where a quiet
gesture at method belongs. Quiet. A wink, not a paragraph.

## Framing rules for the case studies

An entry is an **overview with a hook**, not a write-up. Historical grounding
plus enough intrigue to want the conversation — and then it stops. Roughly a
standfirst, three short paragraphs, and the numbers.

- Lead with the **system**, not the screen. What was incoherent, and what made
  it coherent.
- Name the **upstream** move: where the real problem turned out to sit. This is
  the scope signal, and it is worth one sentence, not a section.
- Numbers are **business consequences**, not activity. "24 interdepartmental
  processes streamlined" beats "40 screens designed".
- **Leave the detail out.** Roles, research method, workshops, artefacts and
  stakeholder narrative are conversation material. Writing them down spends the
  reason to talk.
- **One quoted line from the research, where there is one.** It is evidence
  rather than narration, so it does the work of a process section without
  being one — and it is the user’s voice, not the designer’s, which is a
  harder thing to fake. One per entry, never two.
- End early. The last line should open a question, not close one.
- Keep the working voice for the margin. The published voice stays declarative.
